# Hearthlands source files

Every source file with what it is for, grouped by part. Written by `tools/file_list.py` from the comment at the
top of each file; run it again after adding files. For how the parts work together, read
`docs/SOURCE_GUIDE.md`. Besides these 295 files there are 2204 pictures in `textures/` (made by
`tools/make_textures.py` and `tools/make_scenery.py`, or your own) and 79 sounds in `sounds/` (made by
`tools/make_sounds.py`) and 10 pieces of music in `music/` (made by `tools/make_music.py`). Godot's
`.import` files next to them are made by the editor.

| `scripts/sim_arms.gd` | 352 | Build 66: tools and weapons are made of bronze, iron or steel, and the better the metal the faster and longer they work. | `scripts/sim_market.gd` | 266 | Market prices that move (build 53). Every good has a price in gold: its worth (GameData.res_value) times a factor (Sim.prices) that follows what all realms together have in store: goods that are short grow dear (up to MAX_F times their worth), goods everyone has plenty of grow cheap (down to MIN_F). Once a minute each factor moves a quarter of the way to where supply puts it; selling pushes a price down at once, buying pushes it up. A realm with a market sells at SELL of the price and buys at BUY of it (Market window, M). Merchants (events) and the worth of goods in trades between realms follow the same prices. Rivals with a market sell what they have too much of and buy stone and planks when short. Sim.prices and Sim.price_hist (the last HIST minutes) are saved with the game. |

He leads the first column of five or more that sets out while he is at home, and helps it win its fights (GEN_DUEL a duel for each level). | `scripts/features/clothing.gd` | 171 | Summer and winter clothes. Needs "Seasons, biomes and weather" for the temperature. Every realm keeps a wardrobe: how many of its people own a fur coat and how many own linen clothes. Whenever coats or linen are in store and not everyone has them, people take them (one piece dresses 3 people). Clothes wear out only while worn: coats in the cold, linen in the heat. Below 8 C people without a coat work slower and eat more, the more so the colder it is; above 24 C people without linen clothes work slower. Diseases (if switched on) read cold_need(). |
| `scripts/features/contentment.gd` | 274 | Contentment: how happy a realm's people are, from 0 to 1. Half (0.5) is where every realm starts and changes nothing. Taverns serving beer and bread to the homes near them, and chapels burning candles near them, raise it; hunger and sickness lower it. Content people work faster (up to 10%), unhappy ones slower (down to about 7%). A tavern with beer and bread to spare holds a festival now and then: everyone works 10% faster for 3 minutes. When the realm stays unhappy for 3 minutes, idle peasants start to leave, one a minute. |
| `scripts/features/diseases.gd` | 344 | Diseases, their prevention and their cure. Every ten seconds each disease may break out in a realm; how likely depends on its causes (cold without coats, crowding, too few wells, hunger, heat, wetlands) and on prevention (bathhouses keep people clean, wells give clean water, coats keep the cold out). Once in, it spreads among the healthy and the sick slowly get better by themselves, faster with medicine: medicine in store cures a few at home, and an infirmary cures 8 people with each medicine. Some of the sick die. Sick people don't work, so every building of the realm works slower by the share of its people who are sick. Uses the seasons (temperature, wetlands) and clothes features when they are switched on. |
| `scripts/features/events.gd` | 627 | Random events: every four to seven minutes something happens to each realm, good or bad: a bumper harvest, rats in the granary, a fire, a mine collapse, a travelling merchant, refugees, a festival, a lucky find, wolves, a skilled newcomer, storm damage, bandits, a wandering scholar, an epidemic. Rivals get them too. Switch them off in Settings > Game features ("Random events"); each event can also be switched off on its own in the Events list window (F6). |
| `scripts/features/factory_health.gd` | 470 | Factory smoke and health: the troubles of the Industrial age (build 77).  From the Industrial age on, working smelters, steelworks, mills and every workshop with a steam engine fill the air of the homes near them with smoke (data/health.json says which buildings, how much and how far). Smoke thins with distance; a park takes away half the smoke of the homes near it. Homes also want clean water: a waterworks burning coal pipes it to the homes near it.  Each realm's health (0 to 1) falls with the smoke its homes breathe and with homes that lack clean water. Poor health slows work (up to 15%), raises the risk of fevers and dysentery (Diseases), and below half some people die of it. Artisans and nobles of the age want clean water, nobles a park as well, near their homes: what they lack makes them less content (Contentment).  Everything is worked out every 5 game seconds from the buildings standing, and kept in the save. |
| `scripts/features/feature.gd` | 149 | Base class for optional game systems ("features"): production statistics, seasons, diseases and so on. The simulation calls the hooks of every switched-on feature, and a feature changes the game only through them, so each one can be switched on or off in Settings without touching the rest of the game. To write your own, put a .gd file that extends Feature in the mods folder (Settings shows where). See mods_examples/ in the project for a small example. |

| `scripts/features/fires.gd` | 204 | Fires that spread. A fire starts in a building (a random event's fire, or a catapult's stone) and grows. Fire jumps to wooden neighbours within 3 tiles; buildings mostly of stone or brick do not catch, and nor do the headquarters, stores and stone forts. People from working wells within 10 tiles, and the crew of a fire station within 14, run with buckets: the more of them, the faster the fire dies. A fire left alone burns a building down in a minute or two. |
| `scripts/features/fog.gd` | 349 | Fog of war, watch towers and scouts. You see only what your realm can see: your own land, a few tiles around each building, further from military buildings, 14 tiles from a watch tower with a scout inside, and around scouts out exploring. Scouts are trained at the scout lodge. Rivals are not limited by the fog (the computer plays without it). |
| `scripts/features/geology.gd` | 363 | Geologists. What lies under the mountains is unknown until a geologist has surveyed it: ore veins don't show on the map, and mines can only be built on surveyed tiles with the right ore. Send geologist (Industry tab), then click a mountain in or near your land: an idle peasant takes a pickaxe from the store (he brings it back), walks there over land, checks up to 12 mountain tiles around the spot (18 with Prospecting) and plants a marker on each: coal, iron, gold, copper, granite or nothing, bigger for richer veins. | `scripts/features/seasons.gd` | 728 | Seasons, biomes and weather. A year has four seasons of five game minutes each, starting in spring. The map is split into biomes: a cold northern taiga, a temperate middle, a dry southern steppe and wetland patches near water. Each biome has its own weather, which changes every one to two and a half minutes, and its own temperature (season + biome + weather; mountains are colder). Season, biome and weather speed up or slow down outdoor work, construction, and how much people eat. Other features (clothes, diseases) read the temperature through temp_at() and realm_temp(). |
| `scripts/features/statistics.gd` | 409 | Production statistics: for every realm and every game minute, how much of each good was made and used, plus its population, buildings, soldiers and research. The Statistics window (F2) shows charts. |
| `scripts/features/tool_quality.gd` | 223 | Tool quality. Every tool is crude, standard, fine or masterwork. Tools in store are kept as a pool with an average quality. A worker uses his tool every work cycle and it wears out: crude after 40 cycles, masterwork after 180. Then he takes a new one from the stores; if there is none, he works on with a worn-out tool, 25% slower. Better tools make work faster: crude -12%, standard +0%, fine +12%, masterwork +25%. Build 66: a tool is also of a metal (SimArms): bronze lasts 0.6 as long as iron and works 10% slower, steel lasts 1.6 times as long and works 15% faster. | `scripts/features/wildlife.gd` | 572 | Wildlife you can see: deer and boars in and near the woods, hares in the open, wolves in the forests (more in the northern taiga). They wander, keep away from buildings, and have young (not in winter) up to what the land can feed. Hunters must find a real animal in their hunting grounds; each catch takes one, so over-hunting empties a forest for a while. Wolves now and then scare the herds of sheep and pig farms and dairies (the farm stops for a while) and take deer and hares themselves. Fish jump in the fishing grounds. |
